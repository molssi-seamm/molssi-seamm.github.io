Phase 6: the structure of liquids and solutions#

Status: implemented (2026-10-16) as liquid_structure_step (local commit 03c88b5; the GitHub repository is not created yet). Differences from the plan: the g(r) are the step’s own code (MDAnalysis capped_distance) rather than InterRDF, which gives the block errors directly and also the counts without a cell; the solvation shells are the step’s own code (see the revision below); shells and ion pairing use the heavy atoms of each species, so a molecule is in a shell when its coordinating atoms are. Checked on 1 molal NaCl in SPC/E water; the real-data check against science’s run 12197 is still to do.

Goal#

Bring the structural analysis that science and mlff now do in scripts into a SEAMM analysis step: partial radial distribution functions, coordination numbers, ion pairing (contact, solvent-separated and aggregated pairs), the composition of solvation shells, and residence times – with errors, graphs and stored results. The design document’s table of analysis steps describes it as “Structure: partial g(r) and coordination numbers (MDAnalysis InterRDF); solvation shells, ion pairing and residence times through SolvationAnalysis”, reading positions sampled about every 0.5-1 ps.

What it replaces today (campaign 2026-10-16, section 3.4): analyze_liquid.py (g(r)), goo.py (O-O peaks and n_OO), analyze_nacl_md.py and cip_fixed.py (coordination numbers; CIP/SSIP with a fixed 3.5 Å cutoff, because the automatic first minimum picked the solvent-shared peak at 5.21 Å when one was present).

1. The step#

A new plug-in made from the cookiecutter, in the Analysis group, built like the Viscosity step:

  • Where the MD comes from (md source): its own MD in a sub-flowchart, run as replicas (mode A), or the MD that ran before it in the flowchart: the last run or all runs since a given step (mode B), read through _md_runs and md.h5md.

  • What it needs (md_requirements): atomic positions sampled at least every 1 ps (by default; the residence times need finer frames, see 5), and a periodic system for the parts that need a density (g(r), SolvationAnalysis). In mode A it turns the sampling on itself; in mode B the flowchart check reports what is missing before the MD runs, as for Viscosity.

  • Name: “Structure” is taken by structure_step (energy-optimized structures), so a new name is needed; see Decisions.

2. What to analyse: species and sites#

The MDAnalysis Universe comes from MDData.universe(configuration) (seamm_md_analysis), which has the molecules as residues. The step names each species by its formula as SEAMM finds the molecules (H2O, Li, BF4, C3H4O3), and a site is an element of a species (O in H2O, Li, F in BF4) or, for anything else, an MDAnalysis selection string.

  • Pairs for g(r): by default every pair of elements; or a table of pairs of sites.

  • Solute and solvents for shells and pairing: by default every monatomic ion is a solute and every other species a solvent; or given in a table.

3. Radial distribution functions and coordination numbers#

  • Partial g(r) by MDAnalysis InterRDF for each pair, from 0 to the smaller of 12 Å and half the shortest cell width, in bins of 0.02 Å, over the production frames, excluding pairs within the same molecule.

  • Errors from blocks of frames (five by default), shown as a band on the graph.

  • For each pair: the first peak (position and height), the first minimum, and the coordination number n(r) integrated up to the minimum, with its error. Each pair can be given a fixed cutoff instead, and the step warns when the automatic minimum lies beyond the second peak’s usual range (the case that misled analyze_nacl_md.py).

  • Graphs: g(r) and n(r) per pair; a table of peaks, minima and coordination numbers.

4. Ion pairing#

For each cation-anion pair, every cation in every frame is classified, by the anions within the contact cutoff (r1, the first minimum of their g(r), or given) and within the solvent-separated cutoff (r2, the second minimum, or given):

  • free: no anion within r2;

  • SSIP: none within r1, at least one within r2;

  • CIP: exactly one within r1, and that anion bound to no other cation;

  • AGG: an anion within r1 that is also within r1 of another cation.

The fractions are averaged over the frames, with errors from blocks. This is the step’s own code (MDAnalysis capped_distance), so it works for non-periodic systems too (clusters), and it follows science’s practice of fixed cutoffs where the automatic ones mislead.

5. Solvation shells and residence times (SolvationAnalysis)#

For each solute, through SolvationAnalysis’s Solution (its shell cutoffs from the g(r) minima, or given):

  • coordination numbers of each solvent in the first shell;

  • speciation: the fraction of solutes with each shell composition (for example Li(EC)3(BF4) 41 %), the top few printed and all stored;

  • pairing: the fraction of solutes with at least one of each solvent;

  • residence times of each solvent in the shell, off by default: they need frames every 0.1 ps or so, ten times the default sampling, and the step then asks for it.

5 is offered only for periodic systems, since SolvationAnalysis finds its cutoffs from InterRDF, which fails without a cell.

6. Results#

Printed tables and .graph files as in the other analysis steps, and results for variables and tables: per pair, the first peak and minimum and the coordination number (with errors); per cation-anion pair, the pairing fractions; per solute, the coordination numbers and the speciation (as JSON, like Diffusivity’s per-species results). The coordination numbers and pairing fractions can be stored as properties of the configuration, CN O-Li#Structure#<model> and so on.

7. Non-periodic systems#

A cluster has no density, so g(r) and SolvationAnalysis are not offered; the step reports coordination numbers within fixed cutoffs and the ion pairing (4), which use only distances. The step checks the input’s periodicity before any MD runs (mode A) or before the MD before it runs (mode B), as the other analysis steps do.

8. Dependencies and memory#

  • seamm-md-analysis[structure] (MDAnalysis 2.10) and solvation-analysis (0.4.2: numpy ≥ 2.2, pandas, rdkit, statsmodels, plotly – all already in SEAMM installations, which have numpy 2.5, pandas 3 and RDKit 2026.09). Neither becomes a dependency of core SEAMM.

  • Frames are streamed from md.h5md by MDAnalysis; nothing holds the trajectory in memory. The g(r) accumulate over frames.

9. Tests#

  • Synthetic, with known answers: an ideal gas (g(r) = 1 within its noise); a simple cubic lattice (peaks at a, √2 a, √3 a and coordination numbers 6, 12, 8); hand-built cation-anion arrangements, one per class (free, SSIP, CIP, AGG), in periodic and non-periodic cells.

  • H5MD round trip: the lammps_step test data (64 waters), through MDData.universe.

  • GUI: the dialog built headlessly and reset_dialog run over every choice.

  • Run path: a small LAMMPS run of NaCl in water (locally), in both modes.

  • Real data: science’s NaCl(aq) run 12197 (CIP fraction 0.04 with the 3.5 Å cutoff; Na-O CN 5.52, Cl-O CN 6.58) and the G7 water g_OO, against analyze_nacl_md.py/cip_fixed.py and goo.py.

10. Releases#

seamm-md-analysis first if shared code goes there (the pairing classification and the block errors of a histogram, which the dielectric step may reuse); then the new step’s first release, with its docs and its entries on the main documentation site (the Analysis plug-ins page and the status badges).

Revision (Paul, 2026-10-16, during implementation)#

SolvationAnalysis turned out to be GPL-3 (MDAnalysis has been LGPL-3 since 2.8, which is fine for a BSD-3 plug-in). Paul chose to write the solvation-shell analysis in the step instead – per-solvent coordination numbers, shell compositions, pairing and residence times, on the same distance code as the ion pairing – so the step has no GPL dependency. Section 5 and decision 5 below are superseded; the shells also work for non-periodic systems when the cutoffs are given.

Decisions (Paul, 2026-10-16: the recommendations below)#

  1. The name. “Structure” is taken. Recommended: Liquid Structure (liquid_structure_step), which says what it is for; or Structural Analysis, or reviving the 2021 trajectory_analysis_step stub (never released) under its name.

  2. Comparing with X-ray data at the experiment’s resolution (science’s skinner_same_resolution.py: S(Q) to Q_max 21.8 Å⁻¹ with a variable Lorch window, so g_OO is broadened as in the measurement): in this phase, or a follow-on option of the g(r)? Recommended: a follow-on, since it needs reference data and a choice of window per experiment.

  3. Ion pairing: the step’s own classification by cutoffs (4), recommended, since it works for clusters and matches science’s practice; SolvationAnalysis keeps the shells and residence times (5).

  4. Residence times in this phase, off by default (recommended), or later?

  5. solvation-analysis as a hard dependency of the step (recommended: everything it pulls in is already installed), or optional with that part hidden when it is missing?
